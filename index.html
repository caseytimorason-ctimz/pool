PHASE I

Foundation

the table is mechanical

Make the boring stuff automatic. Deliver a straight cue from a platform that repeats, see the line the same way every time, and pocket cleanly. None of this is fixable later — it's the floor everything else stands on.

RUNG 01

Stance & Body Alignment

A base that repeats: feet, hips and head placed so the cue swings on the aim line with zero steering. Most "aiming" misses are alignment misses — your body, not your eye, is usually off the line.

RUNG 02

Grip, Bridge & Tip Contact

Cradle the cue, don't clench it. A relaxed grip lets the cue finish straight; a solid bridge stabilizes the line; precise tip contact is what actually decides spin. Tightening the grip mid-stroke drops the tip and scoops the ball.

PHASE II

Cue Ball Mastery

the table is physics

The cue ball is the whole game. Learn where it goes and why — center-ball reference, tangent geometry, speed, spin, and the small lies (squirt, swerve, throw) that bend the line. This is the long middle where players plateau for years.

RUNG 06

Center-Ball: Stop · Follow · Draw

The vocabulary of position. Know exactly where center, tip-of-follow and tip-of-draw send the ball at a given speed. Master these three before you ever reach for sidespin.

RUNG 07

RUNG 08

Tangent Line & the 90° Rule

Stun → the cue ball leaves on the 90° tangent, perpendicular to the object ball's path, regardless of speed. Reading this line on every shot is the foundation of position and of not scratching.

RUNG 09

The 30° Rule & Natural Angle

A rolling cue ball deflects ~30° for a wide band of cuts (the peace-sign). The path persists down the tangent and curves later with more speed — so the final angle is reliable, but the landing spot shifts with pace.

RUNG 11

Sidespin: Squirt, Swerve & Pivot Aiming

English is for position, not a default. Understand squirt (deflection off the tip), swerve (the curve on an elevated cue), and how back-hand vs front-hand English changes your aim and pivot. Over-spinning is the classic amateur accuracy killer.

RUNG 12

Throw: Cut-Induced & Spin-Induced

Object balls don't leave on the pure geometric line — friction throws them. CIT (from the cut) and SIT (from sidespin) matter most on slow, stunned, half-ball shots. Learn it both to compensate and to use it (gearing outside english cancels throw).

PHASE III

Table Strategy

the thinking game

You can pocket and you can move the cue ball — now choose well. Patterns, clusters, banks and kicks by system, defense, and shot selection. This is where knowledge starts to beat raw shotmaking.

RUNG 13

Position Play Fundamentals

Shape is angles, not spots. Aim for a zone, leave a natural angle into the next ball, and stay on the correct side. The shortest cue-ball path that keeps an angle is almost always the right one.

PHASE IV

Mastery

the mind game

The shots aren't new anymore — the break, the specialty escapes, the game-specific judgment, and the nerve are. This last climb happens in competition, executing the routine you built in Phase I when it counts.

RUNG 20

The Break

Controlled power, not maximum power. A square, level hit and the cue ball squatting near center table beat a big dry smash. In 9-ball, break from the side to pocket the wing ball; track made-ball and cue-ball control as one stat.
